## Welcome to my Blog!

### Intro

As I write this, I have made a conscious decision to quit listening to music for 6 months.

You might ask: Why?

To be honest, I have been addicted. For the last two years, I’ve had headphones on constantly—while traveling, walking, and even during "focused" work. I was listening to music a lot. A lot.

In practical terms, this wasn’t casual listening. I was using headphones for a minimum of 8 hours every single day. That time wasn’t limited to Spotify alone—it included YouTube, Instagram Reels, and even background music while gaming. From the moment I left home to the moment I went to bed, there was almost always some form of audio stimulation running. Silence had basically disappeared from my daily life.

The worst part wasn't just the volume; it was the content. My consumption became repetitive, looping the same tracks hundreds of times. My taste progressively worsened, shifting toward sped-up, high-dopamine TikTok edits designed to hack my attention. I realized the lyrics and beats were hijacking my emotions and dictating my mood. My mind was in an altered state whenever the headphones were on.

On top of that, the constant noise is taking a physical toll on my eardrums. That scares me.

Considering all this, I have decided to initiate a Music Detox. This is an experiment to see how much brain fog is caused by constant audio stimulation and to test if silence can restore my focus and mental clarity. I want to see how far I can push my brain’s potential, and that requires silence.

### The Protocol (Next 6 Months):

* **Spotify**: Logged out completely. This was my main source.

* **YouTube (Desktop)**: I use "Unhook" to hide the feed, comments, and recommendations. It is now just a search bar.

* **YouTube (Mobile)**: Deleted the app. I also paused my watch history to stop the algorithm from feeding me Shorts.

* **Instagram**: Deleted from my phone. I have a strict 5-minute window every Sunday to check messages, ensuring I don't doom-scroll Reels (which are just another source of music).

* **Headphones**: Strictly for emergency office meetings only.

**How will I substitute the absence of music?**
I won't. During traveling, commuting, or exercising, I will not use anything. I will simply be with my brain and my thoughts. I want to see where my mind goes when it isn't being entertained, and just see how this goes.

**What if I get negative thoughts?**
I anticipate this will happen, especially in the beginning. Usually, I use music to drown out weird or sad thoughts. Now, I will face them. I plan to simply observe them without reacting, and see if the silence eventually helps me process them better. And I feel over time these negative thoughts will diminish.

**What about accidental music?**
If I go to a mall or a cafe and music is playing, I won't run away. I simply won't focus on it. The goal is to remove the choice to listen, not to fear the environment.

### My Hypothesis
I am treating this strictly as an experiment. My hypothesis is that by removing the constant dopamine hits from music, my baseline boredom will increase, forcing my brain to seek stimulation through “deep work” (Maths and Coding) instead.

I also believe that silence is necessary for original thought. I expect that without the constant input of external noise, my brain will finally have the space to process information in the background, leading to clearer thinking and the generation of new, unique ideas.

I anticipate the first 2–3 weeks to be difficult, filled with withdrawal symptoms and phantom music (hearing songs playing in your head even when it is silent), but I hope to reach a state of heightened mental clarity and creativity by Month 3.

### Week 1 Update: The Relapse & The Reset

Seven days in.

Days 1–5 were clean. No music. No headphones. No passive audio stimulation. The silence felt uncomfortable at first, but manageable.

On Day 6, I slipped.

It started with one Instagram Reel. Just one. I told myself it didn’t matter. That single dopamine hit reopened the loop. What followed was two days of mild doom-scrolling and background stimulation. Not eight hours like before — but enough to break the protocol.

Here’s what I observed:

- The craving wasn’t for music specifically — it was for stimulation.
- One small exposure lowered my resistance significantly.
- My mind immediately started seeking more novelty.
- The mental clarity I was beginning to feel slightly regressed.

But I logged it.

This is not a streak challenge. This is a behavioral experiment.  
Relapse is data.

And the data showed something important:

> The real trigger isn’t music.  
> The real trigger is access to frictionless dopamine.

So Week 2 will not be emotional. It will be structural.

### Week 2 Strategy: Structural Reset

#### 1. Full Instagram & Games Lockdown

No partial exposure.

- Instagram deleted.
- Logged out completely.
- All games deleted.

I am marking four events in my calendar:

- **Instagram Detox Start**
- **Instagram Detox End (August 2026)**
- **Games Detox Start**
- **Games Detox End (August 2026)**

Until those end dates, I will not touch Instagram or any games.

This removes negotiation from the equation.  
The algorithm and easy dopamine loops no longer get access to me.

### 2. Replace Idle Time With Structured Growth

The relapse exposed a second issue:  
Outside of work, I wasn’t structured enough.

Vacuum invites stimulation.

So instead of “trying not to scroll,” I am replacing that time with three fixed daily blocks:

#### Movement (60 Minutes Daily)

- 30 minutes treadmill  
  - Incline: 10  
  - Speed: 5.5  
- 30 minutes workout

The treadmill will be done in complete silence.  
No music. No podcasts. No videos.

Just me and my thoughts.

If my mind wanders, I will use that time to think about the LeetCode problem I am solving that day or mentally walk through DSA concepts. The treadmill becomes thinking time, not entertainment time.

Goal: Lose weight. Get lean. Look ripped by year-end.

This becomes my non-negotiable physical anchor.

#### Skill Block (90 Minutes Daily)

- 90 minutes of deep work outside my job
- Minimum: 1 LeetCode problem per day
- Focus: DSA mastery
- Long-term goal: My dream company

No background audio. No multitasking. Pure focus.

#### Creative Expansion (30 Minutes Daily)

- 30 minutes of reading (physical book)
- Target: 25 books by end of the year

Not scrolling. Not summaries. Actual reading.

This expands thinking instead of hijacking it.

These three blocks replace the dopamine slot.

No vague “stay busy.”  
No open-ended browsing.

Structure replaces stimulation.

### How I Expect Week 2 To Feel

I expect stronger cravings.

Now that my brain knows stimulation is still available, resistance will likely increase before it stabilizes.

There may be:

- Increased boredom  
- More phantom music  
- Irritability  
- Mental restlessness  

But this discomfort is recalibration.

Week 2 is not about feeling good.  
It’s about reinforcing identity.

The experiment continues.

### The End Game

This experiment is no longer about perfection.

It is about observing patterns, eliminating weaknesses in the system, and recalibrating my baseline stimulation over six months.

I am logging:
- Mood
- Focus levels
- Deep work output
- Physical training consistency
- Relapses (if any)

I won’t be posting daily updates.

But in August 2026, I will return with full data, trends, and an honest breakdown of what changed — cognitively, physically, and emotionally.

Whether the hypothesis holds or collapses, the results will be documented.

See you on the other side.
